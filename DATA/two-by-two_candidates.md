# Two by Two — candidate boards

Generated by `DATA/two_by_two_candidates.py` from `DATA/artist_works_5to25.csv`. Re-run it after editing the theme list.

## How a board is built

Six works are shown; four go into a 2×2 grid.

- Each **row** is one artist.
- Each **column** is one theme.
- So a board needs artists A and B and themes T1 and T2 with all four
  cells filled: A/T1, A/T2, B/T1, B/T2.
- The two leftover works are distractors.

A pair of artists sharing *n* themes yields `n choose 2` possible boards.

## Method, and what it does not know

Themes are matched on **title text**, not on the images. That has limits
worth knowing before trusting any row below:

- A title can be silent about what is visible. An untitled saint holding
  keys is Peter to the eye and nothing to a regex.
- Getty titles describe the *object*, so furniture, silver, porcelain and
  tapestry are filtered out by keyword; a few object titles may survive.
- Sheets of studies and recto/verso drawings are also excluded. They name
  a subject but do not read as one picture of it, so they make poor cards
  in a game about looking.
- **No image URLs are in this dataset.** Every candidate here still needs
  its four images pulled and eyeballed before it becomes a real board.
- Centuries come from `game_artists.md`, which buckets as `14-16C` /
  `17C` / `18C`. Listed below is the bucket holding most of an artist's
  works, plus any second bucket holding a third or more.
- Two themes that collect the same works cannot be the two columns, since
  a player could not say which column a work belongs in. Pairs overlapping
  by more than 15% of their works are dropped.

### Coverage

The source file lists titles only for artists holding **5 to 25** works.
Twenty-six artists in the collection hold more and have no title list —
but every one of them is a manuscript illuminator or scribe (Hoefnagel,
Lauber, the Bedford Master, Simon Bening), so Tier A below is not missing
a major painter. Boucher, at 30 works, is in the file anyway.

### Themes, and what a player sees

| Theme | Visible without knowing anything |
|---|---|
| Virgin and Child | a seated woman holding an infant |
| Crucifixion | a man on a cross |
| Annunciation | an angel addressing a kneeling woman |
| Nativity / Adoration of the Shepherds | a newborn in a stable with onlookers |
| Adoration of the Magi | richly dressed kings presenting gifts to a baby |
| Entombment / Lamentation / Pietà | a dead body being mourned or laid down |
| Last Judgment | tiers of figures rising and falling |
| Pentecost | a gathered group with flames overhead |
| Single saint | one robed figure, often with an attribute |
| David in prayer | a crowned man kneeling, hands together |
| Venus | a reclining or rising nude woman |
| Bacchus | a wreathed youth with grapes or wine |
| Diana / the hunt | a huntress with hounds, or a hunting scene |
| Cupid / Psyche | a winged child, or a couple with one |
| Portrait head or bust | one person facing the viewer |
| Landscape | open country, little or no figure interest |
| Flowers / still life | arranged objects or blooms on a surface |
| Animals | one or more beasts as the subject |

## What the shape of this says

- **One rich Tier A pair exists**, and it is Boucher / Ricci / Rubens
  circling the same mythological material — Venus, Diana, hunts,
  landscape. Those three artists supply most of the multi-board options.
- **Everything else in Tier A is a one-board pair**, and most rest on the
  same two columns: *Portrait head or bust* × *Virgin and Child*, or
  *Single saint* × *Virgin and Child*. Fine for one puzzle, repetitive as
  a series.
- **The collection's real depth is in illumination** (Tier C, 50 pairs),
  which is exactly where authorship is hardest to see. A game about
  looking has to spend the thinner painting material carefully.
- If Two by Two needs more than a handful of boards, the pool has to grow
  past this file — either artists below 5 works, or themes keyed to
  something other than title text.
